# Wave 17 — the scroll goes round, two icons, and a language

## What ran

T6.9, T6.10 and T6.11, on `wave/17-scroll-icons-and-language`: 27 files, +1898 / −194.

**None of the three came from the backlog.** All three were asked for from the chair, in
Portuguese, while the panel was on — the first wave in this project driven by somebody using
the thing rather than by the plan. Task files were written for them, after the fact, to the same
shape as the scheduled ones.

Three decisions were put to the human as a single question each, because none of them was in
the record: where the language lives, how the loop closes its seam, and whether the icons are
SVG or emoji. All three came back as the recommended option.

## What the review found

**Five, and two of them changed behaviour.**

1. **The twitch guard was left calibrated against a design that no longer exists.**
   `worthScrolling` refuses an overflow small enough for two integer roundings to have invented
   it, and its four-pixel floor was chosen under T6.6 as "far below the twenty-odd a genuinely
   hidden row is worth" — deliberately letting 5–20px through, because back then they bought
   5–20px of travel. T6.9 turned the card into a loop, so **any** overflow now walks the entire
   list past the window for as long as the panel is on: a four-row card reporting 106px in a
   101px window would spend a permanent pass to reveal five pixels. The floor is half a row now,
   measured; the flat four survives only for a caller that cannot measure a row. Stated plainly
   in the fix: *"I changed what `--scroll-distance` meant and did not go back to ask who else
   depended on the old quantity."*
2. **`pseudoInk` measured a title that wraps as one unwrapped line.** It laid the probe out with
   `white-space: pre` and gave the answer a single line-height, so a heading that wraps inside
   its own card came back as one rect as wide as the whole string — a collision with whatever
   sits to its right **that does not exist on screen** — and its second line was invisible to
   the check entirely. It measures `getClientRects()` on an inline span in a holder the width of
   the element's content box now: one rect per line, which is the shape the answer actually has.
   The holder is `position: fixed` rather than a child of the card, because a span inside a flex
   container is blockified and stops producing per-line rects.
3. `stress.js` wrote the date with the raw `?lang` value rather than the tag the panel resolves
   to, so `--lang en-GB` would have measured a string the page cannot produce.
4. The language block in `DataPayload.merge` was inserted **between the night window's comment
   and the statement it documents** — in a file where those comments are the record of a bug
   that already shipped once, that misattribution is a hazard rather than untidiness.
5. The plain theme's `words` field landed between `column()`'s doc comment and `column()`.

Findings 1 and 2 are the same shape as this wave's headline and worth naming together: **both
are a measurement or a constant that stayed behind while the thing it describes changed.** The
guard was right for a card that walks and stops; the probe was right for a title that fits on
one line. Neither failed — each answered confidently about a panel that no longer exists.

Two further fixes landed outside the review, from looking at the device: the battery and the
thermometer were read as a pill and an `i` (`9ad09bc`), and the browser checks were found to
share one Marionette port and therefore cannot overlap (`377c4f4`).

## Where the rework went

1. **The seam.** The loop's distance was computed from `scrollHeight` first. Rows carry a border
   between them and none after the last, so a copy sitting inside a pair is one border taller
   than a copy alone — one pixel, once a pass, which is exactly the jolt T6.6's notes ask this
   panel never to make. It is measured off the clone now. *What would have prevented it:*
   nothing in the task; this is the kind of thing a mutation test finds, and
   `check_scroll.py`'s fifth question was written the same afternoon and asserts the pitch
   exactly rather than within a tolerance.
2. **Turning the battery line into a flex row silently took its wrapping away.** The corner is
   capped at 222px and its overflow story is that it *wraps* into the foot the weather card
   reserves for it; a flex row does not wrap by default. `check_layout.py` caught it on the next
   run at 289px of content in a 222px box. No re-entry cost: the harness was the reviewer.
3. **The harness could not see the collision the language change caused**, and this is the
   finding of the wave. See below.

## The two blind spots in `measure.js`

`measure.js` compares *ink* rather than boxes, deliberately, because two sections are allowed to
share a rectangle. Two whole categories of ink were invisible to it:

- **A `::before` has no DOM node**, so `querySelectorAll('*')` had never been shown a card
  title. All four card headings are pseudo elements and always have been.
- **The ink walk started at a section's *descendants*.** A section whose text sits directly on
  the section element contributed nothing at all — `#clock`, `#date` and `#stale-badge`, three
  of the eight sections. The overlap check has therefore never been able to see the clock, the
  date or the badge collide with anything, in the file whose entire purpose is to catch two
  things drawn in one place.

Both were found the same way: a thirteen-letter Portuguese word for STALE was tried, it landed
squarely on top of `TEMPO`, and the harness reported the pass as clean. Fixing the second is
what made the first one's measurement mean anything — with both fixed, even the eight-letter
replacement at 20px was reported as a 4×18px overlap, which is why the badge is 17px now.

One detail that cost a debugging pass: **Firefox computes the `font` shorthand to the empty
string on a pseudo element.** The probe span that measures a title's width copies the longhands
instead; with the shorthand it measured the text in the document's default face, produced a
plausible number, and answered quietly wrong.

*What would have prevented it:* nothing in the task files. The lesson is about the harness's
own coverage — `measure.js` had a comment at the top describing the T5.4 bug it was built for,
and no statement anywhere of **which ink it can and cannot see**. It has one now.

## What is not proven

- **The loop has not been watched for a full pass on the device.** The seam is asserted to the
  pixel in Firefox; whether sixteen seconds a row reads as scenery rather than as motion from a
  chair is the manual check.
- **Only two languages ship**, and the badge-versus-title constraint is enforced only by running
  `check_layout.py --lang` for each. A third language nobody measures will overlap silently —
  which is now at least *possible* to catch, and was not before this wave.
- Verified by command: `make check`; the five browser checks across both themes and both
  languages; the release APK installed and the panel photographed in pt-BR with both icons.
