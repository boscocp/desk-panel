# Wave 19 — the moon, the jump, and four hypotheses that were wrong

## What ran

T6.13, T6.14 and T6.15, on `wave/19-the-moon-the-jump-and-the-loop`, PR #24. All three came from
the chair, in Portuguese, with the panel lit — the session ran from the evening of 2026-09-21
into the small hours of the 22nd, which is why the note is dated a day after two of the tasks.

None of the three was in the backlog and none of them was what it looked like at the start:

- **T6.13** began as "draw a moon instead of a sun after dark" and ended as a permanent moon on
  the card, its phase fetched from the US Naval Observatory. Both changes were asked for
  mid-implementation, and the icon was sent back three times — once for its shape, twice for its
  size.
- **T6.14** began as "there is a jump" with no cause and ended as a replaced anti-burn-in table
  and a deleted core rule, both of which contradicted a written argument and were therefore put
  to the human before being touched.
- **T6.15** was added after the moon was on screen: the chance of rain, aligned into a column
  with it. Sent back twice — the icon was too small, and a bare drop "fica parecendo umidade".

## What the review found

**Nine, all applied, none declined.** One was reproduced by the reviewer and is the most useful
finding this project has had for a while:

1. **A new test passed on this desk and failed in two other timezones.** `TZ=Europe/Lisbon` and
   `TZ=Pacific/Chatham` both failed `offsetFor does not put the panel in the same place at the
   same time tomorrow`, while `America/Sao_Paulo` and UTC were green — so `make check` was clean
   here and red elsewhere.

   And the property it asserted was **genuinely false**, not merely mis-tested. The 22-step
   table visits two positions twice, a day advances the phase by ten steps, and two days is a
   step of −2 — so for the four phases that land on a repeated index the *offset* after two days
   really is the same one. The assertion is now on the cycle index, which advances every day
   without exception, plus the two things that matter on screen: the panel is not where it was
   yesterday, and it visits at least nine distinct offsets across the eleven days. Verified in
   five zones including two with 45-minute offsets.

2. **The mean-synodic fallback was cached as a success for six hours.** `moon_phase` swallowed
   every failure and returned the arithmetic itself, so `TimedCache` recorded it with
   `stale = False` and held it for the full interval — and the `or synodic_phase()` at the call
   site was dead code, because a producer that cannot fail cannot leave the cache empty. A PC
   that boots before its router is all it takes, which is the exact case `TimedCache.fresh_at`'s
   own docstring was written for. It raises now and the caller falls back.

3. **The moon fetch was on the request path and the payload is all-or-nothing.** `upstream`'s
   timeout is 10s, `DataPoller`'s is 5s, and `DataPayload.merge` returns null if either body
   fails — so a slow USNO would have dropped the whole payload for that cycle, quotes and fx and
   crypto and an already-fresh weather with it, to fetch the thing the provider's own docstring
   calls the least important on the panel. It uses the same background-refresh pattern the
   sparkline histories already had, and there is a test that fails if the fetch is ever awaited.

4. **The inverted night assertion did not cover the way it would actually regress.** With core
   removing nothing, the realistic reintroduction is `animation-play-state: paused`, which leaves
   the animation *named* — so a name-based check passes a card frozen all night. It reads
   `animationPlayState` as well now, and also compares the night animation against the day's.
   Mutation-tested: adding the paused rule makes it fail.

5. **The resume section named a done task as "Next".** It is the next wave's prompt, so that one
   sentence would have misdirected the wave started from it.

6. **The terminator was drawn on the wrong limb around the full moon.** The buckets are an eighth
   of a lunation wide, so `full` arrives with `illum` as low as 96% and covers 1.85 days either
   side; the name-based waxing test flipped the lit limb for the half of that bucket where the
   moon is still growing. It derives from `ageDays / lunationDays` now. This is the one error on
   the card a reader can catch by looking up, which is the whole reason the phase is drawn.

7–9. **Half-updated arithmetic and stale numbers in the comments that justify the design**: "a
   day is 360 steps" beside "1440 mod 22", "ten three positions a night", "239 of every 240
   calls", "one run in seven", "every four minutes" in four files. Every conclusion survived the
   change and none of the numbers did. The class of fault is the one waves 17 and 18 were each
   caught by — a measurement that outlived the thing it measured — arriving for the third wave
   running, which is now less a coincidence than a habit.

The reviewer also confirmed by reading `DataPayload.java` that the moon riding inside `weather`
does reach the page with no Java change, and independently re-derived the `moonPath` geometry and
all four properties of the new burn-in table.

## Where the rework went

**This is the wave's real content, and it is unflattering.** A live bug report — "câmbio e cripto
pararam de girar" — was chased for most of the session through four hypotheses, three of them
built and deployed, before the cause turned out to be one line the panel had always had.

In order:

1. **`prefers-reduced-motion`.** The CSS invites it: `theme.css` disabled the scroller under it
   with a comment saying "the device will never set this". Measured on the device: `matchMedia`
   answers false, and forcing `animator_duration_scale` to 1.0 changed nothing. Dead.
2. **`var()` in the `animation` shorthand.** Plausible, and a real engine difference in general.
   Replaced with the four longhands, built, installed, measured: no change. Reverted rather than
   shipped, because a change with a confident comment and no measured effect is the thing waves
   17 and 18 were both punished for.
3. **CSS animations not working in the WebView at all.** The evidence looked overwhelming —
   `animationName: none`, `animationIterationCount: 1`, `getAnimations()` empty, and the same
   element moving perfectly when driven by `element.animate()`. A whole task file was written
   (T6.14, since deleted), and an implementation with it: the loop moved to the Web Animations
   API, a motion gate went into `host.js`, three browser checks were adapted, `THEMING.md` was
   rewritten. **All of it was discarded**, unmerged.
4. **The actual cause**: `data-night=on`. Every measurement had been taken after 22:00, inside
   the panel's configured night window, where `css/style.css` removes every CSS animation with
   `animation: none !important`. That single rule explains every observation, including the two
   that looked most like engine bugs — the inline style failed because `!important` beats it, and
   WAAPI worked because the cascade cannot touch a WAAPI animation.

**The attribute that governs motion was never read until the end**, and it had already been seen
and walked past: a Firefox probe printed `night: true` in the middle of the session and the line
was not acted on. The lesson is not "measure more" — a great deal was measured, carefully, and
most of it was the wrong thing. It is **read the switch before the mechanism**: the panel has
three documented rules that can stop an animation, and checking those three costs one probe.

Two cheaper things fell out of that expensive detour and are kept:

- the user's own report was right and the record's framing was wrong — "it worked before" was
  about the clock, not about a PR;
- the seam hypothesis for the *other* complaint was ruled out by measurement rather than by
  argument (the pitch logged at a constant `94.00` for six minutes), which is what made the
  burn-in shift findable.

The other rework was ordinary and cheap: two constants in the USNO window were wrong in the
direction that hides itself (the window could end before now, so the API path would silently
never fire), and the moon's first shape was measured on the device as unreadable at 22px.

## What is not proven

- **Burn-in relief is an argument, not a measurement.** Twenty positions in 22 minutes against
  seven in 28 is more coverage per hour, and every position is now held one minute instead of
  four. Nobody here can measure AMOLED wear. ADR 0008 should be re-read if this is revisited.
- **The night motion rule is gone and the cost is real.** A PC left logged in overnight now has
  a card walking in a dark room. That was the owner's call, made with the trade-off stated, and
  there is no switch to put it back.
- **The moon's southern-hemisphere convention is a constant, not a setting.** Every phase would
  be mirrored on a panel moved north of the equator.
- **`source: "mean"` has not been seen on the device.** The fallback is unit-tested against a
  dead upstream, a junk body and a window that misses, but the real server has answered `usno`
  every time it has been asked here.
- **The 34px moon is held up by `check_layout.py` and nothing else.** It spends four of the
  card's 6.6px of slack; the stress pass on all three variants is the only thing standing
  between it and an overflow in a language nobody looked at.

## Cost

Not measured, as ever. What can be counted: **roughly ten build-and-install cycles spent on the
scroll that needed none**, one task file written and deleted unmerged, one implementation written
and discarded, one device setting changed and restored, and nine review findings applied. Prompts
typed by the human: around twenty, most of them corrections or new asks rather than answers —
including three that changed a task's design mid-flight, two rounds of "make the icon bigger",
and one, *"mas vc n ja mediu? vai medir de novo?"*, that was a fair challenge to a measurement I
was about to repeat needlessly.

The single most valuable thing the human did was report what they saw and keep reporting it when
the explanation did not match. "Estava funcionando antes" was right, and the framing I built
around it was wrong.
