# Wave 39 — T4.6, the panel sleeps with the display, and T9.4, private traffic

The owner asked, in Portuguese, whether the phone's screen could turn off when the PC's screen
turns off from inactivity. After the answer, the instruction was "faz isso junto com a proxima
wave e me diz se ja pode abrir o repo publico". The next wave by the record was T9.4, so the
wave is two tasks: a new one, T4.6, written for the request, and T9.4 as recommended.

## What ran

**T4.6**, [ADR 0020](../adr/0020-the-panel-sleeps-with-the-display.md).

- `server/display.py` reads the session's display: Windows through `GUID_CONSOLE_DISPLAY_STATE`
  on a hidden window, macOS through `CGDisplayIsAsleep` over every online display, and Linux
  through Mutter then `xset`.
- `/ping` carries `display`, and the phone has an `IDLE` state with `state=idle`.
- **One design correction came before the first commit.** The Linux readers fork, and inside
  `/ping` two one-second forks would have exceeded the phone's 1.5 s timeout, which reads as a
  logout. They moved onto a sampling thread.
- **Measured:**
  - The macOS reader on the owner's Mac.
  - The phone half on the Redmi, against the real server with its display answer read from a
    file: `state=idle` then `screen=sleep`, and back to `screen=wake` on the next poll.
- Later tries of `pmset displaysleepnow` were undone within the same second by `UserIsActive`
  from a wireless HID receiver. The display was behaving; the reader was not at fault.

**T9.4**, [the spike](../spikes/2026-panel-tls.md), then [ADR 0018](../adr/0018-the-panel-traffic-is-private.md).

- Design 1 shipped: TLS on 8778 with `X-Panel-Key`, `/ping` kept plain, and `/app` off by
  default.
- **The spike's real finding:** macOS's LibreSSL writes P-256 keys with explicit curve
  parameters, and Android's BoringSSL answers every handshake with `decode_error`.
  - Capping the server at TLS 1.2 did not change the phone's answer. That ruled out the
    protocol, which macOS's own curl had pointed to.
  - `asn1parse` showed the explicit parameters.
  - `ec_param_enc:named_curve` fixed both clients on TLS 1.3, and a test pins it.
- The owner's desk was put back in open mode afterwards. The Windows PC has no certificate, and
  a private APK needs every listed PC to be private.
- The history sweep flagged the wave's own test key as a random blob. It was replaced before
  anything was pushed.

Results: `make check` exit 0, server tests 550, and `check_secrets.py --history` clean over 1391
blobs.

## What the review found

One fresh-context agent reviewed both features, read-only, and found nothing serious. It also
ran a real TLS listener. Five stalled TCP connects did not block a keyed request, HEAD and POST
without the key got 401, and plain HTTP sent to the TLS port logged one line and no traceback.
It found five minor items, and all were applied:

- **IDLE → OFFLINE logged a second `screen=sleep`** for one dark stretch. `PanelService` now
  skips the window when the panel is already dark.
- **An idle cycle waits out a second, powered-off PC** on every poll, up to 3 s. The ADR's
  "within one 2 s poll" was wrong in that case and now says so.
- **Windows reads the console display, not the user's session**, and a monitor switched off at
  its own button is invisible. Both are now in ADR 0020's consequences.
- A javadoc in `PcPoller.read` said the opposite of the code.
- An absolute `PC_CERTS` path failed as "not a PEM certificate". It now fails as "absolute".

## What is not proven

- **The Windows display reader has never received a real event.** Its decoding is unit-tested,
  and its ctypes were read against the Win32 headers by review. Neither is a monitor timing out.
- **The GNOME reader** is in the same position.
- **T9.4 on the owner's desk.** It is off, pending the Windows certificate. There has been no
  packet capture from a third device, no press over TLS from the phone's buttons, and nothing
  on Windows.
- **A second pinned certificate** has not been exercised on the device.

## Cost

One owner prompt for the wave. Tokens were not measured. There was one subagent, the review.
About a third of T9.4's time went to the `decode_error`, and most of that to the wrong lead.
