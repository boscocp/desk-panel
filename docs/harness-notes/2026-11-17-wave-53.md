# Wave 53 — A lost overlay grant is logged when it goes, and shown on the panel

The owner found the phone dark on 2026-11-17. The evening before, they had left the Mac, gone to
the PC and shut it down, and the panel "did not switch to the PC and never came back to the
Mac". They asked what happened and whether it needed a wave, then said go ahead.

## The investigation

The Mac server's access log answered the first half. The phone had stopped asking the Mac at
18:29 and 20:44, which is ADR 0022's priority switch to the PC. At 23:53 it came back to the Mac
at the idle cadence (one probe to the Mac, then a 1.5 s timeout on the PC), and `pmset` showed
the Mac's display asleep since 20:49. So it did switch, both ways, as designed. At 09:23:46 the
Mac's display came on, and the phone moved to the 2 s online cadence the same minute. The
screen stayed dark.

`adb` was `unauthorized` until the owner accepted the prompt. Logcat then showed `state=online`,
`wake=retry` and `overlay=denied` every 30 s, and no `screen=wake` from 16:19 the day before
until the owner pressed the power key at 10:01. A WebView update had killed the app at 15:49
(`stop com.google.android.webview due to installPackageLI`). At 16:19 the watchdog revived the
service, and `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` was `ignore`. `dumpsys appops` dated the last use of the
grant to 11-06 12:35 and the first refusal to 11-16 16:19. It was neither hibernation nor
auto-revoke (`app_hibernation` false, `AUTO_REVOKE_PERMISSIONS_IF_UNUSED` default). The grant
was set back with `appops set` before any code was written.

The record's `Next:` (waves 50–52) was "find what switches it off". It cannot be found after the
fact, because nothing on the phone keeps the moment. So this wave does what can be done: log the
moment, and put the loss on the panel while it is still lit.

## What ran

T4.15 on `wave/53-overlay-warning`, PR #92. `OverlayGrant.change` is plain Java. `PanelService`
watches `OPSTR_SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW`, re-reads the grant on every battery broadcast, and logs
`overlay=lost`/`overlay=back`. `DataPayload.fold` adds `"overlay": false` to the battery object.
`batteryFields`, `formatBattery` and `batteryLineClass` put `sem sobrepor apps` in red on both
themes. ADR 0026, TESTING, INSTALL-PHONE, README, and the TT.6 guard were updated.

Measured on the Redmi, twice (before and after the review's fixes). The install reset the grant,
`overlay=lost` landed at the service's first reading, and the red note showed (screenshot). Then
`appops set ... allow` logged `overlay=back` within the 2 s the acceptance allows, and the note
went. The task's device block exits 0. `./gradlew test` is green, and web is 179/179.

## What the review found

Four fresh-context cells: conventions (this repo's `CLAUDE.md`, since the BHub lens is Python),
claim verification, test sabotage, and Java/JS correctness. All four delivered. **3 🔴 and
about 17 🟡, all applied except where noted.**

- 🔴 **`publish()` handing the grant to `withOverlay` was untested.** Sabotage replaced it with
  `true` and the suite stayed green. It is now `DataPayload.fold`, with a JVM test that "no
  reading yet" is not a loss. Re-mutated: it dies.
- 🔴 **The plain theme's red was untested.** Removing `lost ? 'hot' :` passed 178/178. It is now
  `batteryLineClass` in `format.js`, with a test. Re-mutated: 2 failures.
- 🔴 The STATUS section cited this note before it existed. Closed by this file.
- 🟡 Found by three lenses independently: `overlayGranted` is static and was not reset in
  `onCreate`, so a service restarted in the same process never logged `overlay=lost`. It is now
  reset next to `dormant`.
- 🟡 Teardown removed the posted check before stopping the watcher. The order is reversed, and
  `checkOverlay` returns for a dead instance.
- 🟡 The backstop sat after the battery receiver's early return, so "every broadcast" was false.
  It now sits before the return.
- 🟡 Claims: "runs on a binder thread" and the 500 ms race were stated as fact, and the Android
  docs say neither. They are now worded as a precaution. Also fixed: "no new timer", "four"
  against "five" losses, "the words match the setting's name", the neon-only wording for both
  themes, and a CSS comment that credited source order for what specificity does.
- 🟡 The task's device block grepped for `overlay=lost` without starting the app, which an
  install does not do (ADR 0025). It now starts it.
- 🟡 The TT.6 single-source guard never covered `overlay=` or `wake=`. Both are in it now, with a
  filter for comment lines.
- 🟡 Wave 49's note read `appops time=` as the last change. ADR 0026 now quotes today's line and
  the `dumpsys` entry showing it is the last access. Wave 49's note was left as written: a note
  records what was believed then.
- 🟡 Test placement: the marker literals moved to `MarkersTest`, and the `withOverlay` cases to
  `DataPayloadTest`.

## What is not proven

- **The cause of the revocation.** This wave makes the next one leave a timestamp. Nobody has
  read the log around a real, unprovoked `overlay=lost` yet.
- **`PanelService` itself has no JVM test.** Passing `true` in place of `overlayGranted` at the
  call site would still pass. The device block is what covers it.
- **The neon note's red is checked only by eye**, from a screenshot. No test loads a theme.
- **The `AppOpsManager` callback was proven only through `appops set` from adb.** Whether a
  MIUI-side revocation fires it is exactly what the next loss will show. If it does not, the
  battery backstop catches it within one broadcast.
- The note wraps onto a second line in neon and sits over the top of the spectrum bars. It was
  left that way on purpose.

## Where the rework went

- The first version put the decision "a null reading is not a loss" in the Android class. The
  repo's own rule (keep logic out of Android classes) would have caught it, and sabotage did.
- Three lenses found the static-reset bug, which `dormant`'s comment, three lines away, describes
  word for word. A precedent in the same method is the first thing to copy.

## Cost

Three prompts from the owner: the report, accepting the adb prompt, and "go ahead". Tokens and
wall clock were not measured.
