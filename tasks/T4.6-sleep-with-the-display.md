# T4.6 — The panel sleeps with the PC's display

Size: M · Prereqs: T4.3, T4.5 · Files: `server/display.py`, `server/server.py`,
`server/config.example.toml`, `server/config.example.json`,
`android/app/src/main/java/dev/bosco/deskpanel/PcState.java`, `PcHosts.java`, `PcPoller.java`,
`PanelService.java`, `Markers.java`, `docs/adr/0020-the-panel-sleeps-with-the-display.md`

Requires: the phone on adb and a reachable PC, for the manual check

## Goal

Asked for on 2026-09-30: "can the phone screen turn off when the PC screen has turned off too,
from inactivity?" A PC left logged in with its monitor asleep still answers `/ping`, so the
panel stayed lit in front of nobody. [ADR 0020](../docs/adr/0020-the-panel-sleeps-with-the-display.md)
is the decision: the PC reports its display, and the phone sleeps on "off".

## Steps

1. `server/display.py` reads the session's display: Windows through `GUID_CONSOLE_DISPLAY_STATE`
   on a hidden window, macOS through `CGDisplayIsAsleep` over every online display, and Linux
   through Mutter's `PowerSaveMode` then `xset q`, sampled off the request path. Every failure is
   `"unknown"`.
2. `/ping` answers `{"ok": true, "display": ...}` when the server has an app. `follow_display`
   in `DEFAULT_CONFIG` and both examples, on by default.
3. `PcState` gets `IDLE`: not online, the online cadence, and dormant on battery.
4. `PcHosts.displayOff` reads the body, and only the literal `"off"` counts. A cycle
   prefers a PC with its display on, and it is idle only when none has one.
5. `PcPoller` reads the `/ping` body (capped) and hands `PanelService` the state.
   `PanelService` logs `state=idle` and takes the offline path.

## Manual check

- **On the Mac (done 2026-09-30):** `pmset displaysleepnow` turned `CGDisplayIsAsleep` to 1
  within half a second, and user activity turned it back. A wireless HID receiver on this desk
  woke the display within the same second on later tries, which is the display behaving, not
  the reader.
- **On the phone (done 2026-09-30):** against the real server with its display answer taken from
  a file, "off" gave `ping=idle`, `state=idle`, `screen=sleep`, and "on" gave `state=online`,
  `screen=wake` on the next poll.
- **Not yet run:**
  - On the Windows PC, let the monitor time out and watch `curl http://<pc>:8777/ping` say
    `"display": "off"`, then move the mouse and watch it say `"on"`.
  - The same on a GNOME desktop.
  - Across both PCs, check that a dark Windows PC beside a lit Mac keeps the panel lit.
