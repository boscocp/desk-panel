# T5.5 — Blank the screen when the device runs hot

Size: M · Prereqs: T4.3, T4.4, T5.4 · Files: `MainActivity.java`,
`android/app/src/main/java/dev/bosco/deskpanel/ThermalState.java`, `web/js/app.js`,
`web/js/format.js`, `web/test/format.test.js`, `web/css/style.css`

Requires: phone (on adb), and a way to warm it — a charging session under load, or
`adb shell dumpsys battery set temp <tenths>` to inject a temperature

## Goal

Act on the one measurement ADR 0008 already takes. The panel spends its life plugged in, in a
stand that restricts airflow, painting a bright image for hours — and the largest heat source it
controls is the screen it is holding on. Today it can only warn.

See [ADR 0012](../docs/adr/0012-thermal-screen-cutoff.md), which amends invariant 3 to make
temperature a second authority over the screen.

## Steps

1. **`ThermalState.java`** — plain Java, no Android imports, same rule as `PcState`
   ([ADR 0006](../docs/adr/0006-no-framework-web-layer.md)'s argument applied to this layer, and
   `android/CLAUDE.md`'s "keep logic out of Android classes"). It owns the hysteresis and nothing
   else:

   - `record(tempC, nowMs)` returns whether the thermal verdict changed
   - blanks at **45 °C**, clears at **38 °C**, and holds its verdict in between
   - deterministic and injectable: no clock read, no sleep, no thread. Time comes in as a
     parameter, as it does in `PcState`

2. **Arbitration in one place.** The screen is on only when `PcState` says online **and**
   `ThermalState` says not too hot. Write that as a single expression somewhere obvious, not as
   two conditionals in different callbacks that happen to agree. Do not fold thermal state into
   `PcState`; the PC poller has no business depending on a battery broadcast.

3. **Blank with the ADR 0005 fallback, not real sleep** — `screenBrightness = 0f` plus a black
   render, keeping the activity foreground so it can bring the screen back itself. Real sleep is
   for the PC-offline case, where waking is driven from outside. If T4.4 concluded that MIUI
   cannot wake the screen reliably and the fallback is now the mechanism for both, say so in the
   commit and keep the markers distinct anyway.

4. **Markers**: `screen=thermal` when blanking, `screen=thermal-clear` when restoring. Distinct
   from `screen=sleep` / `screen=wake`, which stay PC-driven. TT.6 owns the marker vocabulary —
   extend it there rather than inventing a second scheme
   ([ADR 0009](../docs/adr/0009-testing-strategy.md)).

5. **The colour ramp.** `format.js` gains a pure `tempClass(tempC)` returning `"normal"` /
   `"warm"` / `"hot"`, with a test. `app.js` applies it to the DEVICE card's temperature. Yellow
   from the T5.4 warning point (**40 °C**), red approaching the cutoff. The ramp is the piece
   that makes the blanking legible before it happens — by the time the screen goes black it has
   been visibly reddening, so a dark panel is not mistaken for the PC having gone away.

6. Keep the DEVICE card small and in a corner, as T5.4 step 3 already asks. This task makes its
   temperature reading louder, not its card bigger.

## Acceptance

```bash
docker compose -f docker/compose.yml run --rm build ./gradlew test
! grep -q "android\." android/app/src/main/java/dev/bosco/deskpanel/ThermalState.java
node --test "web/test/**/*.test.js"
! grep -qE 'document\.|window\.' web/js/format.js
adb logcat -c
adb shell dumpsys battery set temp 460
sleep 15
adb logcat -d -s DeskPanel | grep -q 'screen=thermal'
adb shell dumpsys battery set temp 300
sleep 15
adb logcat -d -s DeskPanel | grep -q 'screen=thermal-clear'
adb shell dumpsys battery reset
adb shell dumpsys power | grep -q 'mWakefulness=Awake'
```

`dumpsys battery set temp` takes tenths of a degree, so 460 is 46 °C and 300 is 30 °C — both
outside the 38–45 band, so neither depends on where the hysteresis is holding when the test
starts. The final two lines matter as much as the rest: `battery reset` undoes the injection, and
the wakefulness check proves the screen genuinely came back rather than the marker being emitted
by a path that left it dark.

**Write the hysteresis test so it would fail on a single-threshold implementation.** Drive
`ThermalState` up through 45, back down to 40, and confirm it is still blanking — a naive
implementation clears at 40 and passes any test that only walks the temperature in one
direction.

## Manual check

Warm the phone in its stand and watch the DEVICE temperature go yellow then red before the
screen blanks. The point being checked is that the sequence is legible: the panel tells you it
is getting hot before it goes dark, so a dark panel is never ambiguous.

## Notes

- **Do not add a timeout anywhere.** Invariant 3's "never by a timeout" is untouched by ADR
  0012. Temperature is a measured condition, delivered by a push broadcast (T5.4 step 5) — do
  not poll it.
- The thresholds are constants, not config. They are about what the hardware tolerates, not
  about what the owner wants shown, and `config.json` is documented as the place for display
  choices.
- Yellow and red are the third and fourth exceptions to the pink-on-black palette, after green
  for gains. They are admitted on the same argument: semantic, conventional, and used for
  nothing else.
- `adb shell dumpsys battery set temp` sticks until `reset`, including across app restarts. A
  forgotten injection looks exactly like a real thermal problem.
