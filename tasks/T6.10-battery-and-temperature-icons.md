# T6.10 — The battery line draws its two labels

Size: S · Prereqs: T5.4, T6.8 · Files: `web/js/format.js`, `web/themes/neon/theme.js`,
`web/themes/neon/theme.css`

Requires: node for the arithmetic; Firefox for the corner

## Goal

Asked for 2026-09-21, from the chair: *"o bat da bateria pode virar um icone de bateria tbm? e
a temp ter um icone de temperatura, ou algo relacionado?"*

The corner read `BAT 87% · 31°C`. Four characters of the widest thing on that line were spent
saying what a picture says at a glance, on a panel whose whole argument is that it is read in
passing.

## Steps

1. `batteryFields(battery)` in `format.js`: the line taken apart into `{level, temp, unplugged}`,
   or null when there is nothing worth a corner. `formatBattery` composes its string out of it,
   so the two cannot disagree about what the line says — and `plain`, which is undesigned on
   purpose, keeps using the string.
2. Two icons in `neon`, in the same 24-unit stroke-only box as the weather glyphs, so they take
   the card's ink and inherit the temperature bands' colours (T5.4) for nothing.
3. The battery draws its own charge as a filled bar inside the outline.
4. `unplugged` stays a word.

## Acceptance

```bash
node --test "web/test/**/*.test.js"
python e2e/layout/check_layout.py
python e2e/layout/check_layout.py --theme plain
python e2e/layout/check_night.py
```

## Manual check

From the chair, at about 50cm: the battery reads as a battery and the thermometer as a
thermometer, and the charge bar agrees with the number beside it.

## Notes

- **The fill is more than the ask, and it is what makes the icon worth its space.** A static
  outline beside a two-digit number is a label; a filled one is read before the number is. It
  is the same information twice, which is what a sparkline already does beside a price. Drop
  the `<rect>` in `renderBatteryIcon` and the icon is a plain outline again.
- **The bar is drawn to the body's *inner* edge, not to the path.** A 1.6-unit stroke sits half
  outside the line it is drawn on, so a bar drawn to the path's own coordinates disappears
  under its own outline and reads as one solid lozenge above about 80%.
- **`unplugged` is still a word on purpose.** There is no picture for "this phone is now running
  its own battery down" that a stranger reads the way they read a battery outline, and that is
  the one thing in this corner that matters (ADR 0014).
- **It took three goes at the size, and two at the drawing.** 14px read as a tick rather than
  an object. 18px put the icons at the cap height of the digits beside them — where the eye
  expects a *label* to sit — and both were reported as hard to read from the chair anyway.
  They are 22px now, slightly taller than the digits, which is the point: these are pictures,
  and a picture that has to be *resolved* rather than recognised needs more room than a letter
  of the same nominal size. T6.3's type floor does not apply; it is about letterforms.
- **Two drawings had to change as well, and the reasons are worth keeping.** The battery's
  charge ran to the outline's inner edge, so above about 60% it met the outline and the whole
  icon read as a solid lozenge — a pill, not a battery. There is a visible gap on every side
  now, at 1% and at 100% alike, and that gap is what makes the shape read as a container with
  something in it. The terminal went from a hairline stroke standing a pixel and a half off the
  body — invisible at this size — to a filled rounded rectangle with air around it, because the
  nub is half of what a battery is recognised by. And the thermometer's outline bulb with a
  hairline through it is a keyhole or a lowercase *i* at 18px: it has a **filled** bulb and
  column now, which is what mercury looks like, and three scale marks down one side, which
  nothing else on the panel has.
- **Turning the line into a flex row took its wrapping away**, and `check_layout.py` caught it
  at once: 289px of content in a 222px box, with `unplugged` 52px outside the viewport. The
  corner's whole overflow story is that it wraps into the two-line foot `#weather` reserves for
  it rather than running out over the CRYPTO card's prices, so the row needs `flex-wrap: wrap`
  and `justify-content: flex-end` — a flex container does not pass `text-align` to its items.
