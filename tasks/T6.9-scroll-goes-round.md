# T6.9 — The overflowing card goes round, at a quarter of the speed

Size: S · Prereqs: T6.6 · Files: `web/js/format.js`, `web/themes/neon/theme.js`,
`web/themes/neon/theme.css`, `web/themes/plain/theme.js`, `web/themes/plain/theme.css`,
`e2e/layout/check_scroll.py`, `docs/THEMING.md`

Requires: node for the arithmetic; Firefox for the loop

## Goal

Asked for 2026-09-21, from the chair: *"a rotação tá mt rápida, diminui pra 1/4, e deixa
rotação infinita, com os valores subindo em loop."*

T6.6 gave an overflowing card a slow walk down to its hidden rows and back. Two things about
that were wrong once it was living on a desk: it was four times too fast, and it came back.

## Steps

1. **A quarter of the speed.** `SCROLL_SECONDS_PER_ROW` goes from 4 to 16, and each theme's
   `--scroll-seconds-per-row` with it. The two numbers are comparable because speed in pixels
   per second is `rowHeight / secondsPerRow` under either design.
2. **A loop, not a there-and-back.** The theme renders the list **twice** and the animation
   moves the scroller by exactly one copy. At the end of a pass the second copy is sitting
   where the first started, so the restart is invisible and the rows appear to rise for ever.
3. **Measure the pitch, do not compute it.** The distance is
   `children[rowCount].offsetTop - children[0].offsetTop`, not the scroll height of one copy:
   rows carry a border between them and none after the last, so a copy inside a pair is one
   border taller than a copy alone.
4. `linear infinite`, no `alternate`, no holds. `--scroll-moving-fraction` described how much
   of a pass the keyframes spent holding at the two ends; a loop has no ends, so it goes, and
   `scrollPlan` loses its fourth argument.
5. A pass is now `rowCount * secondsPerRow` rather than `hidden * secondsPerRow / fraction`:
   the card travels the whole list, so two cards showing nine rows move at the same speed
   whether one row is hidden or five.
6. `check_scroll.py` grows a fifth question — the seam.

## Manual check

From the chair, at about 50cm: the card rises steadily, never jolts, and never reverses. The
seam should be impossible to find by watching for it.

## Notes

- **The seam is the only part of this that can be wrong by one pixel and look right in every
  frame but two**, which is why `check_scroll.py` asserts the pitch *exactly* rather than
  within a tolerance. A tolerance there would be a tolerance on the jolt itself. It was
  mutation-tested from both sides: a computed pitch two pixels short, and a second copy whose
  rows differ from the first's.
- **Clone after measuring the overflow, never before.** The second copy is what would make a
  card that fits look like it overflows, and `applyScroll` is the only place that knows which
  is which. The clones are thrown away with everything else on the next refresh, because
  `renderList` empties the scroller before it rebuilds.
- The clones carry the pulse class their originals had, which is right: either copy may be the
  one on screen when a value changes.
- `plain` keeps a different number from `neon` — 24 rather than 16 — because its rows are two
  lines tall and a row there is worth more reading time. The quarter is the ratio, not the
  constant.
- **The twitch guard had to be re-derived, and the review found it.** `worthScrolling` refuses a
  card whose overflow is small enough to have been invented by two integer roundings — four
  pixels, chosen under T6.6 as "far below the twenty-odd a genuinely hidden row is worth",
  which deliberately let 5–20px through because back then they bought 5–20px of travel. They
  do not any more: under the loop, *any* overflow walks the whole list past the window for as
  long as the panel is on. A four-row card reporting 106px of content in a 101px window would
  have spent a permanent pass revealing five pixels. The floor is half a row now, and the flat
  four survives only as the answer for a caller that cannot measure a row.
