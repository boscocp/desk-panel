# T6.3 — Legibility on the physical device

Size: S · Prereqs: T6.1, T2.2 · Files: `web/css/style.css`

Requires: phone (on adb), and your eyes at 50 cm

## Goal

Close the gap between "looks right in Chrome" and "reads well on the actual phone at arm's
length". They are never quite the same.

## Steps

1. Install and put the phone where it will actually live, in its stand.
2. Sit where you actually sit. Roughly 50 cm.
3. Adjust, in this order: type size, then weight, then contrast, then spacing.
4. Check it at a glance — this panel is read peripherally, not studied. If finding the price
   takes a deliberate look, the hierarchy is wrong.
5. Check in daylight and in a dark room. Neon on black behaves very differently in each.

## Acceptance

The machine half is that the sizes actually changed and the page still parses:

```bash
node --check web/js/app.js
! grep -qE 'font-size:\s*1?[0-9]px' web/css/style.css
```

No font under 20px survives this task; anything smaller is what fails the 50 cm test.

## Manual check

Every card legible from about 50 cm without leaning in, judged **on the device**, not in a
browser at desk distance. This one is irreducibly human and deliberately so.

## Two things T6.1 measured and handed forward

**Fix a dim secondary by lightening `--accent-dim`, never by brightening `--accent`.** The
palette deliberately makes the secondary *less saturated* rather than darker, so the smallest
text on the panel measures more contrast than the clock does: `#BB81AA` is 6.83:1 on black
against `#D53FA7`'s 5.10:1. If the card titles still read dim at 50cm, raise the secondary's
lightness. Raising the accent instead closes the gap from the wrong end and flattens the
hierarchy the whole palette depends on.

The titles (`B3`, `FX`, `CRYPTO`, `WEATHER`, `DEVICE`) are the smallest text on the panel and
the most likely thing here to fail. A contrast ratio cannot settle it; that is what this task
is for.

**The 20px floor above costs more room than it looks, and the amount is known.** Measured with
`e2e/layout/check_layout.py --extra-css`, which tries a size change without editing `web/`:
raising the card titles from 11px and the date and battery line from 16/17px to 20px overflows
`#quotes` by exactly **1px** (content 303x131 in a box of 303x130). So the floor is very nearly
affordable, but not free. The room is in `#panel`'s `1.22fr / 1fr / 1fr` row split, in the
`5px 10px` card padding, or in the 3px title margin — one of those, not a smaller font.

Re-measure with that harness after every size change here. It is the check that would have
caught T2.3's clipped fifth card, and it takes about twenty seconds.

## Notes

- The Redmi Note 10 panel is 2400x1080 at about 6.4 inches, so it is dense. Sizes that look
  generous in a desktop browser come out small.
- Resist shrinking things to fit more in. A panel showing four things clearly beats one showing
  eight things you have to squint at.
