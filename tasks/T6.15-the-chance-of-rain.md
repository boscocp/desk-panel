# T6.15 — The chance of rain, beside the city

Size: S · Prereqs: T6.13 · Files: `server/providers_openmeteo.py`, `server/tests/test_providers.py`,
`web/js/format.js`, `web/test/format.test.js`, `web/themes/neon/theme.js`,
`web/themes/neon/theme.css`, `web/themes/plain/theme.js`, `web/themes/plain/theme.css`,
`web/js/mock.js`, `e2e/layout/stress.js`, `e2e/layout/night.js`

Requires: Firefox on `PATH` for the layout checks. Nothing here needs the phone.

**Asked for from the chair on 2026-09-22**, with T6.13's moon already on the card: the chance of
rain as a percentage beside the city, right-aligned so it lines up with the moon's percentage
below it, **no word, with a rain icon**.

First question was whether the data exists, and it does — checked against the live endpoint
rather than the docs, because the answer is not where you would look for it:

    GET api.open-meteo.com/v1/forecast?...&daily=precipitation_probability_max
    {"daily": {"time": ["2026-09-22", "2026-09-23"],
               "precipitation_probability_max": [98, 92]}}

**`current` has no probability field at all.** It carries `precipitation`, `rain` and `showers`
— millimetres that have already fallen — and no forecast. The probability is a `daily` field and
an `hourly` one.

## Goal

A drop and a percentage at the right-hand end of the city's line, in a column with the moon's.

## Steps

1. **`precipitation_probability_max` joins the `daily=` list** in `fetch_forecast`, and
   `normalise` picks it with the same matched date as the temperatures — never `[0]`. Without
   the timezone parameter open-meteo cuts the daily arrays on UTC days, so after 21:00 in São
   Paulo index 0 is already tomorrow, and a chance of rain from the wrong day is a wrong number
   rather than an error.

2. **The day's maximum, not the current hour, and the two are not close.** Measured at
   2026-09-22T00:00 in São Paulo: the day's max was 98% and the hour ahead was 31%.

   The day, for two reasons. This card is already day-scoped — the min and max beside it are
   today's — and a panel read at a glance must not change the scale of its numbers between one
   line and the next. And the hourly series answers "should I leave now", which is a question
   a panel with no interaction cannot be asked.

3. **`chanceOfRain(weather)`** clamps to [0, 100] and rounds. Absent is **null, never zero**: a
   server too old to send the field has not forecast a dry day.

4. **`formatPercent` replaces `formatIllum`.** T6.13's formatter and this one had the same body
   and the same `--%` decision, and two copies is two places for that decision to drift. The
   two numbers sit in a column on the card and must not be formatted differently.

5. **The drop is floated, and that is the whole of the layout.** The first cut made `.w-city` a
   flex row with the drop at its far end, and the stress pass failed it: `SAO JOSE DOS CAMPOS`
   wraps to two lines at 20px, the drop and `100%` took about 60px of the card's 204, and with
   144px left the city needed a third. The card ran 201px of content into a 180px box and the
   range line was drawn over the AGENDA card below — at seven of the burn-in cycle's offsets.

   A float takes width out of the **first line only**, so the city flows beside it there and
   uses the full column underneath. The card keeps its height whatever the city is called, which
   is the one thing it cannot negotiate: its whole argument is that the city is server config.

   The float has to come **before** the text in the DOM. A float only displaces inline content
   that follows it.

6. **The plain theme writes it out.** `rainChance` is therefore wording and not only an
   accessible label — the neon theme was asked for without a word and uses it as the drop's
   `aria-label`; plain has no pictures on purpose and the word is the whole of what it has.

The last line is the one that would rot: it asserts the probability comes from the day
`current.time` names and not from index 0, which is the difference between today's forecast and
tomorrow's on any evening after 21:00.

## Manual check

On the device: the drop and its percentage sit directly above the moon and its percentage, both
hard against the card's right edge. That column is the half of the request a measurement cannot
check — `check_layout.py` says the content fits, not that two numbers line up.

## Notes

- **98% is a real forecast and it looked wrong.** On the evening this was built the day's max was
  98 while the sky was merely cloudy, which is exactly the confusion the daily/hourly choice
  creates. It is the correct number for the question "will it rain today"; if the panel ever
  wants "is it raining now", that is `hourly` and a different card.
- **No unit is drawn.** The drop says what the number is about and `%` says what kind of number
  it is. That was the ask and it is also what keeps the card readable in a language nobody has
  translated it into.
