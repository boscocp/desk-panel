# T6.11 — Every word the panel shows, in the panel's own language

Size: M · Prereqs: T3.12, T6.7, T6.8 · Files: `web/js/format.js`,
`web/themes/neon/theme.js`, `web/themes/neon/theme.css`, `web/themes/plain/theme.js`,
`web/js/mock.js`, `server/server.py`, `server/config.example.toml`,
`server/config.example.json`, `DataPayload.java`, `e2e/layout/*`

Requires: node; Firefox for both languages; the Android toolchain for the payload copy

## Goal

Asked for 2026-09-21, from the chair: *"o weather ficou bacana com o ícone, o que seria esse
light drizzle?"* — and then, having been told: *"os textos que aparecem tem que ser localizados
pra no futuro ter uma opção de língua, deixar por enquanto padrão pt-br mas com opção pra mudar
pra inglês."*

The panel stands on a desk in Brazil and said `Light drizzle`, `WEATHER`, `STALE` and
`unplugged`, under a date its own `toLocaleDateString` was rendering in whatever language the
host happened to prefer.

## Steps

1. A table per language in `format.js`: the WMO conditions, the four card titles, the stale
   badge, the word for a phone on its own battery, and the tag Intl gets for the date.
   `pt-BR` and `en` ship; `pt-BR` is the fallback.
2. `language` in `server/config.toml`, riding `/quotes` into the payload beside `theme` and
   `night` — so changing it is editing a file on the PC and never a rebuild (ADR 0013).
   `DataPayload.merge` copies it, with a test, because that is exactly what T6.4 forgot.
3. Both themes read `payload.language` at the top of `render()` and keep the table for the
   length of that render.
4. The card titles stop being literals in the stylesheet: `content: attr(data-title)`, written
   by the theme. `::before` is kept because `renderWeather` empties `#weather` on every payload
   and a real `<h2>` would go with it — which is why the title was a pseudo element to begin
   with.
5. `?lang=` in `mock.js` and both layout fixtures, and `--lang` on `check_layout.py`.

## Manual check

From the chair: every word on the panel is Portuguese, including the date. `language = "en"` in
`server/config.toml` and a restart of the server turns all of it English without touching the
phone.

## Notes

- **English stays everywhere else, and that is not an inconsistency.** Code, comments, docs and
  commit messages are English because they are read by whoever works on this; the fifteen
  strings on the glass are read by whoever owns it. Those are different audiences and this is
  the only place they differ.
- **The fallback is pt-BR, not English.** The same decision `host.useTheme` makes about an
  unknown theme: a typo in a file on the PC must cost nothing anybody can see, rather than
  switching the whole panel to a language its owner did not ask for.
- **The device's own locale is deliberately not what decides.** A phone in a stand running its
  system in one language is not evidence about who is looking at the panel, and there would be
  no way to ask for the other one.
- **`DEFASADO`, not `DESATUALIZADO`.** The badge shares its strip with the weather card's title
  and neither word is fixed any more; at thirteen characters it landed on top of `TEMPO`. The
  badge also came down from 20px to 17 — the title is read every minute of every day and the
  badge is on screen for minutes a month, so the badge is the side that gives. The constraint
  that is left is real: the longest card title and the badge have to fit the weather card's
  width between them, and a third language has to be checked against it.
- **`na bateria`, not `desconectado`**, which in Portuguese reads first as a network having
  dropped — the wrong alarm entirely on a panel whose other states are about the PC being away.

### The harness could not see either collision, and that is the bigger finding

`e2e/layout/measure.js` compares *ink* rather than boxes, because two sections are allowed to
share a rectangle. Two whole categories of ink were invisible to it, and this task walked into
both at once:

- **A `::before` has no DOM node**, so `querySelectorAll('*')` had never been shown a card
  title. It measures them now with a probe span carrying the pseudo's computed font, which is
  the only way to get a width for text that has no element.
- **The walk started at the section's *descendants*.** A section whose text sits directly on
  the section element — `#clock`, `#date` and `#stale-badge`, three of the eight — contributed
  no ink at all. The overlap check has therefore never been able to see the clock, the date or
  the badge collide with anything, in the file whose entire purpose is to catch exactly that.

Both were found by trying a thirteen-letter word for STALE, watching it land squarely on
`TEMPO`, and being told the pass was clean. Fixing the second one is what made the first
one's measurement mean anything.
