# T1.1 — Clock in plain HTML/JS

Size: S · Prereqs: none (does not need the container) · Files: `web/index.html`,
`web/js/app.js`, `web/css/style.css`

## Goal

The smallest possible panel: a running clock, opening straight from the filesystem in Chrome.
This is the thing T2.2 will render on the phone as the first sign of life.

## Steps

1. `web/index.html` — document shell, links the stylesheet and `js/app.js`. No framework, no
   CDN, no build step ([ADR 0006](../docs/adr/0006-no-framework-web-layer.md)).
2. `web/js/app.js`:
   - render time and date, updating once per second
   - define `window.onPcState(online)` and `window.onData(payload)` as no-op stubs. The native
     layer calls these, and they must exist from the start so later tasks only fill them in.
3. `web/css/style.css` — background `#0A0118`, cyan `#00E5FF`. Full styling comes in T6.1;
   here just make it dark and readable.

## Acceptance

```bash
node --check web/js/app.js
! grep -rqE '\bfetch\(|XMLHttpRequest|EventSource|navigator\.sendBeacon' web/
grep -q 'onPcState' web/js/app.js
grep -q 'onData' web/js/app.js
```

The negated grep is invariant 1 ([ADR 0002](../docs/adr/0002-native-owns-network-io.md)),
asserted rather than trusted. The two positive greps prove the native bridge's entry points
exist from the start, which is the whole reason they are stubbed here.

## Manual check

Open `web/index.html` in Chrome: the time updates every second and the console is clean.

## Notes

- Use a monospace or tabular-figure font for the clock, or the digits jitter as they change.
- Do not add a `fetch` anywhere in `web/`. Ever. That is invariant 1 — see
  [ADR 0002](../docs/adr/0002-native-owns-network-io.md).
