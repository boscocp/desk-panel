# T6.14 — The jump, and the loop at night

Size: M · Prereqs: T6.2, T6.4 · Files: `web/js/format.js`, `web/test/format.test.js`,
`web/css/style.css`, `web/js/host.js`, `e2e/layout/check_night.py`,
`e2e/layout/check_layout.py`, `docs/THEMING.md`, `docs/TESTING.md`

Requires: Firefox on `PATH` for the browser checks; the phone on adb for the manual half.

Two complaints from the chair on 2026-09-21, with the panel lit. Neither was a bug: both were
the panel doing exactly what it was written to do, and in both cases what it was written to do
was decided for a panel this is not.

## 1. "Tem uma hora que dá uma pulada"

**Measured before anything was changed.** Eleven screenshots, 28 seconds apart, comparing the
clock block's position: between t=84s and t=112s the **whole panel** moved (-8, +2) physical
pixels in one frame. That is the anti-burn-in shift — `BURN_IN_AMPLITUDE_PX = 4` every
`BURN_IN_STEP_MINUTES = 4` — and it is instantaneous by design.

It was not the scroll's seam, which was the first guess. The pitch was logged on the device for
six minutes and measured once per card at exactly `94.00`, never changing, so the loop never
restarted. Writing that down because the seam is the expensive thing to go looking at.

The table's own comment argued for the big steps: *"a slow raster leaves each position adjacent
to the last, which is the least relief per move"*. True of one move and the wrong quantity —
what spares an AMOLED is how long any single pixel stays lit across hours, which is coverage
over time. The trade-off was put to the human in those terms and the one-pixel path was chosen.

**The new table is 22 steps over 20 positions and every step moves exactly one pixel, the wrap
included.** Three things about its shape are arithmetic and each one cost an attempt:

- **5 wide by 4 tall, not 5 by 5.** A grid graph is bipartite, 5x5 splits 13/12, and a closed
  tour of an odd number of nodes on one cannot exist — so the wrap would have been a 3px jump,
  the exact thing being removed.
- **22 steps, not 20.** A closed tour of the band is 20 positions, a step is a minute, and 1440
  is 20 × 72: the panel would stand at the same offset at the same time every day. The test
  `offsetFor does not put the panel in the same place at the same time tomorrow` has asserted
  that property since T6.2 and 20 would have broken it silently.
- **The two extra steps are a one-pixel detour**, so no step grew to buy the cycle length.

The means end up 0.14px off the bands' midpoints rather than exactly on them. A table that hit
them exactly needed 26 steps and a dwell three times longer on some positions than others,
which is worse for the thing the shift exists to do.

## 2. "Volta loop o tempo todo"

`css/style.css` removed every animation under `[data-night]`, so at 22:00 the cards stopped. The
owner watched that happen, reported it as a fault, and asked for the motion back.

The rule's argument was a promise about the room: peripheral vision is more sensitive to motion
in the dark, so a card giving up its hidden rows at 03:00 is the one thing here that can wake
somebody. **What it did not weigh is invariant 3.** This panel is lit *only* while the PC is on
and logged in; when the PC goes away the screen sleeps. The dark room the rule protected has
somebody awake at a machine in it.

Removed, with the reasoning left where the rule was. The cost is real and is the owner's: a PC
left logged in overnight now has a card walking in the dark.

Two things fall out of it that are worth naming:

- **The blackout rule is untouched.** Offline or over the thermal ceiling nobody can see the
  panel and a moving card only costs battery. That one was never about taste.
- **The value pulse fires at night now.** `animation: none` had suppressed it entirely, which is
  why the rule was never `paused` — a paused pulse freezes mid-flash and leaves one number at
  `--accent-hi` until morning. Removing it has neither problem.

## Steps

1. Replace `BURN_IN_OFFSETS` with the 22-step one-pixel path and set `BURN_IN_STEP_MINUTES` to 1.
2. Assert the table's four properties in `web/test/format.test.js` rather than describing them:
   every step is 1px including the wrap, all 20 positions appear, the cycle length does not
   divide a day, and each axis's mean is within a fifth of a pixel of its band's midpoint.
3. Rewrite the daily-drift test to compute when the phase returns — `cycle / gcd(1440 % cycle,
   cycle)` — instead of hardcoding it, so editing the table cannot quietly turn 11 days into 1.
4. Delete the `[data-night]` animation rule and put the reasoning where it was.
5. **Invert `check_night.py`'s question 2 rather than deleting it.** A card that stops at night
   is now a regression, and an assertion nobody inverts is the one that hides a change.
6. Follow the stale claims: `docs/THEMING.md`, `docs/TESTING.md`, `check_layout.py`'s sweep
   comment and `host.js`'s split-of-responsibility comment all said "every four minutes" or
   "nothing moves at night".

`check_blackout.py` and `check_pulse.py` are in the list because step 4 deletes a rule that
applied to everything animated: they passed before by testing a panel where nothing moved at
night, and have to still pass testing one where things do.

## Manual check

On the device, after `assembleRelease` and `adb install -r`: watch the panel for five minutes
and the step is no longer something you catch. Verified the same way the fault was found —
screenshots 28 seconds apart, comparing the clock block's position — because a 1px step is
below what the eye reports reliably and that is exactly the claim being made.

## Notes

- **The burn-in relief is now a different shape, not obviously a better one.** Twenty positions
  in 22 minutes against seven in 28 is more coverage, and every position is held one minute
  instead of four. Nobody here can measure AMOLED wear; the argument is coverage over time and
  it is an argument, not a measurement. ADR 0008 should be read again if this is ever revisited.
- **The night window still exists and still dims the panel.** Only the motion half went. An
  owner who wants the old stillness has no switch for it, which is a thing this task chose not
  to build rather than a thing it overlooked.
